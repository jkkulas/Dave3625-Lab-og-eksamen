Welcome to DAVE3625 – Introduction to Artificial Intelligence
Hello everyone, and welcome to the class of DAVE3625 – Introduction to Artificial Intelligence!

Artificial intelligence can sometimes appear remarkably intelligent and, at other times, surprisingly limited. In this course, we will explore both the potential and the limitations of AI. The purpose of the course is to help you understand how common AI methods work, how they are applied in practice, and how to evaluate AI systems critically. By the end of the course, you should be able to look beyond the hype and make informed judgments about what AI can and cannot do.

Aim of the course
This is a foundation-level course that provides a broad introduction to artificial intelligence and its main subfields. Course page:

https://student.oslomet.no/en/studier/-/studieinfo/emne/DAVE3625/2026/H%C3%98STLinks to an external site.

You will learn about:

Key AI concepts, terminology and historical developments

Data preparation and the role of data in AI systems

The difference between artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI

Supervised and unsupervised machine-learning methods

Regression, classification and clustering

Model training, testing and evaluation

Generative AI and large language models

Ethical and societal implications of AI

The current capabilities and limitations of AI systems

The course combines theoretical lectures with practical exercises. We will use Python, Jupyter notebooks, Pandas and scikit-learn to experiment with machine-learning methods. The course does not teach Python programming from the beginning. You should therefore have basic Python programming skills before starting the practical work. The programming exercises are intended to help you understand AI methods rather than make you an expert Python or AI developer.

Guest lectures
The course may include a maximum of two guest contributions. Any guest contribution will be directly connected to the topics and learning outcomes in the teaching plan. It will supplement the scheduled curriculum and will not introduce separate examinable material that is not covered by the course resources.

Reading materials
The principal course materials are:

Elements of AI, particularly the chapters on the definition of AI, machine learning, neural networks and the implications of AI (https://www.elementsofai.com/)

Andrew Ng, Machine Learning Yearning, with selected chapters on development and test sets, evaluation, error analysis and machine-learning strategy (book in reading list)

The latest AI Index Report, with selected sections and figures announced through Canvas (reading list)

Lecture slides, practical notebooks and additional course notes published in Canvas

Assignment descriptions and supporting material published in Canvas

Students are not expected to read every part of these resources. The relevant chapters or sections are identified in the teaching plan. Additional details will be provided together with each lecture.

Practical sessions (labs)
Labs normally take place on Wednesdays following the Tuesday lecture. They are intended to reinforce the theoretical material through programming, model experimentation, interpretation and discussion.

The exercises may include:

Data exploration and preprocessing

Regression and classification

Decision trees, K-nearest neighbours and support vector machines

Model evaluation and comparison

K-means and hierarchical clustering

Simple artificial neural networks

Evaluation of generative-AI systems

Responsible-AI case studies

Mandatory assignments
There will be three mandatory group assignments:

Assignment 1: Data preparation and introductory supervised learning

Assignment 2: Classical machine learning and model evaluation

Assignment 3: Generative AI, evaluation and responsible use

The assignments are not graded, but all three must be approved for a student to qualify for the final examination.

Assignments are normally completed in groups of two to four students unless otherwise specified. Detailed requirements, deadlines, submission instructions and approval criteria will be published separately in Canvas.

Final note
Artificial intelligence is one of the most exciting and rapidly evolving fields of our time, but it is not magic.

During the semester, you will learn to look beyond impressive demonstrations and exaggerated claims. You will examine how AI systems learn from data, why they make mistakes, how their performance should be evaluated, and when their use may create ethical or societal concerns.

I hope the course will help you develop both practical confidence and a critical understanding of artificial intelligence.
